An author-named centring target (#164)

Both figures below are the same construction, built from the element strings of view/round_geometry/desargues.html applet 3 — Desargues' theorem in the hyperbolic plane, in Dr. Joyce's original 1996 coordinates. Only the centerOn option differs.

The subject of this figure is the Poincaré disk: everything that matters happens inside it. But the construction also contains circumcircles through near-collinear points, and a hyperbolic geodesic is a large circle — so the figure's bounding box is several times its canvas no matter where the points are put. #162 keeps the worst of them from defining the frame, which stops the figure landing off-screen; it cannot make the bounds-derived centre be the disk's centre, because that is not what the bounds describe.

Press m on each canvas (or use the maximize control) — the centring transform only applies in the maximized view. On the left the disk drifts off toward one side; on the right it sits in the middle of the frame.

A misspelled target is not swallowed. The third canvas asks for centerOn: "diskcentre" — the British spelling, which no element answers to — so it reports through the #154 diagnostics (watch for the badge) and falls back to bounds centring rather than silently doing nothing.
