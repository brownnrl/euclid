Point.followPath — watch a point travel (#137)

A.Point.slide answers "put this slider at that value". This answers "watch this point travel", and by default it brings the point back to exactly where it started — the trip is the illustration, not a change to the figure.

The motivating case is I.31. The construction's hidden hypothesis is that the apex A stays on one side of DC; drag it across and the mirror intersection is selected, so the "parallel" stops being parallel. The deck could only invite a manual drag. Press p and advance — slide 2 walks A across DC and back on its own.

  • Slide 2 — { via: [[250, 250]] }: A crosses DC, the construction reacts every frame, and A lands back on its start.
  • Slide 3 — { via: ["C"], settle: true }: same mechanism but it stays put at the far end.
  • Slide 4 — { arc: { center: "DC" } }: swings A about the midpoint of DC and back. A line centre means its midpoint, and the radius defaults to where the point already is, so nothing jumps on entry.
  • Slide 5 — { arc: { center: "DC", sweep: 270 }, settle: true }: sweep is degrees (default 180); its sign picks direction.
  • Slide 6 — { along: "DC", settle: true }: walks a whole segment end to end.

Press r between runs to restore the figure. Legs are parameterised by arc length, so a long leg takes proportionally longer than a short one.
